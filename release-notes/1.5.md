A meeting no longer costs you a backup. Before a meeting your disks are now unmounted rather than ejected: just as safe to unplug, but if you join from your desk and leave the cable in, the disk is reconnected when the meeting ends and a Time Machine backup it stopped starts again straight away. Back-to-back meetings keep it down until the last one is over.

While it waits, the disk row says when it comes back. ⌘E and eject on sleep still eject fully, because they mean you are leaving.

An encrypted Time Machine disk locks whenever it is unmounted, and only Time Machine holds its key, so Eject Guard asks Time Machine to start a backup to bring it back. If that does not work, a notification tells you to plug it in again.

The disk list keeps to real disks. Disk images are never listed, a disk you do not guard is forgotten 30 days after you unplug it, and right-clicking an unplugged disk now offers Forget this disk. Two disks with the same name show their size, or the start of their UUID, so you can tell which is which.

Eject Guard has its own menu bar icon: an eject triangle over a bar, the app icon in miniature. The bar says what the guard is doing: a thin line when there is nothing to guard, a solid bar while a disk is guarded, and Time Machine's progress, with its clock badge, while a backup runs. When the guard takes a disk off before a meeting, the triangle crouches, jumps out of the menu bar and lands on a dashed outline that keeps the disk's place while it is away. When the disk comes back, the outline fills and the triangle settles onto it. With Reduce Motion on, the icon simply changes.

When a disk will not come off, the warning names the app holding it, or says it could not tell, rather than a line of lsof output.

The command line tool reports reconnects, and `eject-guard` lists the disks waiting for one.
